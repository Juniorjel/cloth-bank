# Project Proposal & Draft: Cloth Bank Management System (कपडा बैंक)

---

## 1. Project Title
**Cloth Bank Management System (कपडा बैंक)**  
*A Web-Based Platform for Clothing Donation, Disaster Relief Logistics, and Campaign Tracking*

---

## 2. Project Overview & Background
Nepal frequently faces severe natural disasters including monsoon flash floods, landslides, and harsh winter cold waves (*Sheetlahar*). In recent climate emergencies—such as the devastating **Rasuwa floods and landslides**—hundreds of families lost their homes and personal belongings within a matter of minutes.

Following any major disaster, access to clean, dry, and warm clothing is an urgent survival necessity right after food and clean water. Without warm jackets, thermals, and blankets, vulnerable groups—especially infants, young children, and elderly citizens—face severe health risks such as hypothermia, pneumonia, and cold-related illnesses.

While thousands of citizens across urban centers have surplus, wearable clothing ready to donate, the traditional donation ecosystem is fragmented, inconvenient, and completely uncoordinated.

**Cloth Bank** bridges this critical gap by providing a modern web and mobile platform that streamlines the clothing donation workflow: offering flexible collection options (Doorstep Pickup vs. Self Drop-off) and providing real-time tracking of relief campaigns for complete transparency.

---

## 3. Problem Statement & Real-World Justification

### A. The Disorganized Traditional Donation System
1. **Ad-hoc & Spontaneous Drives**: Physical donation tents set up on roadsides often lead to chaotic piles of clothes without quality inspection, sorting, or sanitization.
2. **Zero Transparency & Inventory Tracking**: Coordinators cannot determine how many items have been gathered, what specific sizes or categories are missing, or where the aid has been distributed.
3. **Mismatched Aid**: Disaster victims receive random clothing items instead of categorized emergency kits (e.g., kids' thermals, warm blankets, adult winter jackets).

---

### B. The Donor's Dilemma (The "Ram" Scenario)
To understand the necessity of this project, consider the common challenge faced by everyday citizens:

* **The Persona**: Ram is an office employee working a standard **10:00 AM to 6:00 PM** schedule.
* **The Intention**: Ram sees news of the Rasuwa flood disaster. He checks his wardrobe and gathers bags of clean, warm sweaters, jackets, and blankets that could protect a flood-affected family.
* **The Roadblock**:
  - Physical donation collection booths only operate during daytime business hours (10:00 AM – 5:00 PM).
  - The drop-off booth is located on the other side of the city in heavy rush-hour traffic.
  - Ram cannot leave work during office hours or spend 2 hours in commute just to deliver a package.
* **The Result**: Ram is unable to donate, and flood victims in Rasuwa go without vital warm clothing.

---

## 4. How Cloth Bank Solves the Problem

Cloth Bank eliminates all friction for donors and relief organizations through three core pillars:

### 1. Flexible Dual Donation Modes
* **Option A: Doorstep Pickup (For Donors like Ram)**:
  - Donors enter their location on an interactive Google Map and select a convenient pickup time slot.
  - An assigned field logistics agent/driver arrives directly at their doorstep to collect the package.
* **Option B: Self Drop-Off (For Weekend/Commute Donors)**:
  - Donors who prefer dropping off items themselves can view a verified directory of collection hubs, operating hours, and generate an instant digital tracking ID.

### 2. End-to-End Real-Time Tracking & Verification
* Every donation progresses through a transparent status lifecycle:  
  $$\text{Pending} \longrightarrow \text{Assigned} \longrightarrow \text{Picked Up} \longrightarrow \text{Delivered to Hub} \longrightarrow \text{Verified}$$
* Once verified by the hub admin, the donor receives an automated digital **Thank You Receipt** confirming their clothes have entered the relief pipeline.

### 3. Campaign-Specific Counters & Public Accountability
* The public and NGOs can view live campaign counters (e.g., *"1,560 / 2,000 clothing sets collected for Rasuwa Flood Relief"*).
* Organizations can see exactly how much inventory is available across categories (Men, Women, Children, Blankets) to dispatch aid efficiently to disaster zones.

## 5. Key Objectives & Expected Outcomes

| Objective | Target Outcome |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Increase Donation Participation** | Eliminate logistical friction by offering doorstep pickups for busy working individuals. |
| **Rapid Disaster Response** | Enable NGOs to launch targeted disaster drives (e.g., Rasuwa Flood Relief) within minutes. |
| **Transparent Supply Chain** | Live tracking of all collected, sorted, and distributed clothing items. |
| **Empower Field Logistics** | Provide field drivers with a streamlined mobile pickup queue and GPS navigation. |
| **Reduce Textile Waste** | Promote a circular economy and reuse of quality garments in Nepal. |

---

## 6. System Stakeholders & User Roles

1. **Donors (General Public)**:
   - Browse active disaster relief campaigns.
   - Schedule doorstep pickup with GPS map pin or select a self drop-off hub.
   - Track donation status and receive email acknowledgment.

2. **Field Agents / Logistics Drivers**:
   - Access assigned pickup queues on a mobile-friendly dashboard.
   - One-touch phone dialer to contact donors.
   - Update statuses in real-time (`Picked Up` ➔ `Delivered to Hub`).

3. **Admins / Relief Coordinators**:
   - Create, monitor, and manage emergency relief campaigns.
   - Dispatch and assign field agents to donor locations.
   - Inspect and verify incoming clothing bundles.
   - Generate inventory reports for disaster dispatch.

---

## 7. Conclusion
The **Cloth Bank Management System** transforms clothing donation in Nepal from a fragmented, inconvenient process into an organized, technology-driven humanitarian supply chain. By solving logistical challenges for busy citizens like Ram and providing complete inventory transparency for disaster relief drives like the Rasuwa flood response, this platform ensures that **no clothes go to waste, and no disaster victim is left unprotected.**
